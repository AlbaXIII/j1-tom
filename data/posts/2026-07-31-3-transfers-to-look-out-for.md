With the dust now settled from the one-off J1 100 Year Vision League, the top tier of Japanese football prepares to kick off the first year of the new J1 calendar – the very first time that the season has coincided with those of the European leagues. Now, J1 clubs have been able to have that extra layer of squad-building security in their transfer dealings, immune to having their best players plucked out mid-season by Royal Antwerp (miss you [Tsunashima](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pAEvkgDVxEE)) - here are a few of the most intriguing transfers made so far this summer.

## **Anderson Lopes, FW** – Lion City Sailors (SGP) to **Vissel Kobe**
Once most famously known as the guy who [fell down the advertising hoardings whilst celebrating a goal for Hokkaido Consadole Sapporo](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=31poTBZUxXc), during Lopes’ tenure at Yokohama F. Marinos from 2022 to 2025 he blossomed into one of the most explosive and reliable goalscorers in J1, firing them to the league title in 2022 and then leading the scoring charts in 2023 & 2024. In 2025 he made the move to double (or triple) his money in Singapore for the Lion City Sailors, firing them to a domestic double – and it’s no surprise to see how the Marinos have struggled in his absence. Now 32, Kobe have taken the gamble on bringing the mercurial Brazilian back to Japan for an undisclosed fee, and if he can rediscover his Yokohama form then Vissel have hugely increased their firepower in the quest to retake their J1 throne.

## **Ryo Germain, FW** – Sanfrecce Hiroshima to **Shimizu S-Pulse**
It might be a stretch to give Ryo Germain too much credit for the ongoing Hiroshimannaisance (™) but on his day, Germain is one of the most dynamic attacking threats in the entire division. Hajime Moriyasu certainly thought so, and in his 3 appearances for the Samurai Blue he notched 5 goals in 2025 alone. Despite this, recent struggles for Hiroshima with only a modest 3 in 15 during the 100 Year Vision league necessitated a change in scene – and an ambitious S-Pulse will hope to be the beneficiaries of a savvy move for a forward who at 31 is still very much in his prime.

## **Sebastien Haller, ST** – Utrecht (NED) to **Sanfrecce Hiroshima**

[One penalty is all it takes](https://youtu.be/MnVtGdVopbI?si=lSsC438gER6EIYhj&t=23). With his team 0-1 down, Haller’s final-day miss against Mainz 05 in 2023 during his stint at Borussia Dortmund changed the trajectory of what had up until that point been one of football’s most emotive stories of resilience and determination. Breaking through into the European footballing consciousness as part of Eintracht Frankfurt’s potent 2017 attacking trident with Ante Rebic and Luka Jovic, Haller’s star would continue to rise even through a sticky patch in England with the perpetually cursed position of striker at West Ham, and he arrived at Dortmund after an incredibly prolific spell at Ajax in 2021-2022. However, just as his career was reaching its pinnacle, Haller was diagnosed with testicular cancer. 6 months on the sidelines followed, only for a faintly miraculous return during the second half of the 2022-23 season, scoring 9 in 19 as Dortmund closed in on their first title in 10 years to finally, **finally** break Bayern Munchen’s grip on the _Meisterschale_. Alas, it wasn’t to be, and after the final day calamity at home to Mainz, Haller has not been the same player since, with a few underwhelming spells in Spain and back at Utrecht returning 10 goals in 4 years. However, with that said, one only needs to see [what Haller is capable of](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6SO2p6WwVg4) to know that, if that player still exists within the 32-year-old, then Hiroshima have not only got a goalscorer, but a silky yet towering physical presence who makes every player around him better. Knowing what he has gone through, it would be a fitting swansong.

### **Oriol Romeu, MF** – Southampton (ENG) to **Avispa Fukuoka**
It’s unusual for a graduate of La Masia to arrive in Japan without much fanfare, but Romeu is used to doing things under the radar. The 34-year-old has racked up over 400 appearances in the Premier League, La Liga and The Championship doing the sort of dirty work that one would not usually associate with a scion of Barcelona’s vaunted academy. However, Romeu is so effective in that role that the current La Liga champions brought him home in 2023 after he had become one of the most underrated anchormen in Europe during his time at Southampton and Girona. Now very much in the twilight of his career, Fukuoka hope that his footballing IQ has not receded in “old age” and can help the wasps kick on out of mid-table.
