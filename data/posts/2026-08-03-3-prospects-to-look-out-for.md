The pipeline of talent from Japan to Europe has become one of the most exploited over the last couple of seasons, with clubs from all of Europe’s top 10 leagues and beyond realising the potential within the J League. This summer alone, players have made the journey from J1 to tier 1 European teams such as RB Leipzig, Real Sociedad and Le Havre. So what better time to cast an eye over the future stars of Japanese football, sure to be featuring in Europe in the coming years – and in the process becoming ‘that guy’ amongst your friends who know them before they were megastars. Yes mate, saw him at Nagoya Grampus, knew he was mint.
NB - For players to qualify for this list, they must be **21 or under before the start of the 2026/2027 J League** and have not been sneakily bought from under my nose when I started writing this article. Looking at you [Ryūnosuke Satō]( https://www.valenciacf.com/official-statement-ryunosuke-sato).

## **Yumeki Yokoyama (19)** – MF – Cerezo Osaka
Japan has never had a problem producing incredibly gifted, tricky dribblers who are an absolute joy to watch with the ball at their feet. [Daichi Kamada](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pjlx1UhcNks), [Ritsu Doan](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6QGe14MZ9fY), [Takefuso Kubo](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aGmlhlKLfNk) – the list goes on. Yumeki Yokoyama looks to be the next one off the production line. Lighting up the lower leagues with FC Imabari as a young teenager, Cerezo brought him into the J1 limelight for the 100 year vision league and he continued to do what he had been doing for a while – [making defenders look daft](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WwK8eL6sD14), both domestically and for the Japanese National side youth setup. An attacking midfielder of serene grace and balance but with an eye for the decisive, Yokoyama looks every bit the established J1 attacking threat at only 19.  Cerezo look well placed to challenge for major honours this season and Yokoyama will no doubt shine once again.

## **Makoto Himeno (17)** – FW – JEF United Chiba
Of all the ways to make your professional debut for your hometown club, scoring an 83rd minute winner to seal a place in the promotion playoff final has to be up there with the best. [And with a sublime chip to boot](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Fp-KImX5A3Y). Makoto Himeno wrote his name in JEF United folklore by bursting through the RB Omiya Ardija (yes, that RB, extra points there) backline to level the game in the last 10 minutes, setting the stage for JEF’s first return to the top flight in over 10 years. Since then, Himeno’s pace, direct running and attacking threat down the left-hand side caught the eye during the 100 Year Vision League – and even though he is yet to play a  full 90 minutes, became the first and probably only ever recipient of the J1 100 Year Vision League Young Player of the Season. Maybe for another 100 years? 
Still only 17, Himeno will look to build on his incredible promise in a JEF United Team expected to struggle, and it will be an interesting watch to see if he can shoulder the pressure. Either way, I don’t imagine he will be there for long.

## **Sung-jun Yoon (19)** – MF – Kyoto Sanga 
In contrast to the other players on this list, Sung-jun Yoon is more of a lowkey performer. But mark my words, this player is going places (probably soon). He is a real rarity for a young player – a [Marco Veratti-style holding midfielder](https://youtube.com/shorts/gWpiPfsMC-A?si=mLsKeKhiA2YA_2_w), that looks to totally dictate the pace of a game, dominating from just in front of the defence. His vision, engine and eye for a switch mark him out from other prospects, but what is most striking is his ability to read the game – rare commodity in a young player and one that is incredibly coach-able. Do not be surprised to see him plucked from Kyoto soon, but if not, he will almost certainly feature in everything Sanga do well this season.
