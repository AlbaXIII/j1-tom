A weekend of shock results, chaotic games, and an unusual amount of spicy red cards in the J League as the matchdays tick into double figures.
<br>

No sooner as a new leader emerges, the picture changes once again - and there is no other place to start that at **Kashiwa Reysol**, who were somewhat inexplicably well beaten at home by a resurgent **Nagoya Grampus**. Nagoya took an early lead only to be quickly clawed back by the frontrunners, but rather than crumble the visitors - inspired by an imperious Yudai Kimura - scored again either side of half time to secure a 1-3 win. The chasing pack have taken full advantage, with all 3 of **Vissel Kobe**, **Machida Zelvia** and **Sanfrecce Hiroshima** enjoying 3-0 wins of various comfort levels. Kobe visited **Fagiano Okayama** and scored 3 second half goals to roll over somewhat pliant hosts. Machida welcomed **Avispa Fukuoka** and didn't have to break much sweat to turn over the struggling wasps, whilst Hiroshima were very fortunate to not go 1-0 down at home to **Kyoto Sanga** before rallying in the second half with 3 unanswered strikes - and another goal and assist for Akito Suzuki - Sanfrecce now take top spot on goal difference. **FC Tokyo** continued to keep pace with the challengers, demolishing a increasingly doomed-looking (sob) **Tokyo Verdy** in the capital city derby. After Verdy striker Itsuki Someno was sent off in the 18th minute for a high boot, it was one-way traffic punctuated by 4 strikes and another nail in the green coffin. **Kashima Antlers** wasted no time in continuing their resumption of normality by scoring twice in the first 10 minutes to nip any **V-Varen Nagasaki** hopes in the bud - Aleksandar Čavrić & of course Léo Ceará securing (number 12 for the season) a regulation win for the holders. Heartbreak abound for **Mito Hollyhock**, who likewise had a man sent off early (this time Taishi Semba for a wild challenge on Tatsuki Seto) but managed to hold **Urawa Reds** at bay...until the 94th minute, when Shuta Sasa headed in a Takuro Kaneko cross to crush Mito's hopes of escaping with a point. Another early red card was on display at **Cerezo Osaka** with Ayumu Ohata taking an early bath against **Kawasaki Frontale**, but the home team battled valiantly in a gloriously chaotic see-saw encounter, eventually succumbing to a 2-4 loss having held the score at 2-2 until the 74th minute. Lastly, **JEF United Chiba** had a goal disallowed in a 0-0 stalemate at home to **Yokohama F. Marinos**, the visitors second 0-0 in a row. Bodes very well for the visit of Verdy in the week. Is it possible for teams to score minus goals?

## GAME(S) OF THE ROUND

**Gamba Osaka** have been on a horrible run recently. Winless since August, 4 defeats in a row, no goals scored since the 2nd of September. So when they went 2-0 down away to **Shimizu S-Pulse**, the writing was on the wall. However, Tomukazu Myojin's team summoned a heretofore unknown fighting spirit and in the last 10 minutes clawed their way back to a point, with Ryotaro Meshino and Issam Jebali getting on the scoresheet, the latter in the 95th minute. Shimizu had their chances to put it to bed and will be kicking themselves, but the madcap scramble Gamba initiated in the last chapter of the game was great fun, and could kickstart their revival - ahead of a huge trip to Fukuoka in midweek.

## GOAL OF THE ROUND

A lot of stiff competition this week for the best strike - Ryotaro Meshino's volleyed lob to get Gamba back in the game against Shimizu and Yudai Kimura's go-ahead goal for Nagoya away at Kashiwa were both great strikes, but for this week I'm going to go for **Kento Tachibanada**'s thunderbolt for Kawasaki Frontale against Cerezo Osaka. Sometimes there is no greater joy than watching someone kick a ball really really hard.
